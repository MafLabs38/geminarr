# 0017 — The browser is for Geminarr alone, and Geminarr relays its screen behind its own sign-in

> **Amended by [ADR 0019](0019-the-desktop-has-an-origin-of-its-own-opened-by-a-ticket.md) (2026-11-17)**: the relay is no longer under `/browser/` of Geminarr's own origin but on an origin of its own, opened by a ticket of the operator's session. What the relay forwards, and what it keeps apart, stands.

- **Status**: accepted by the maintainer in the conversation of 2026-11-15
- **Amended by**: [0021](0021-geminarr-has-no-debugging-port-to-the-browser.md): Geminarr has no debugging port to the browser any more, so whatever this record says of `CDPURL`, the debugging address, attaching to the browser or driving it no longer holds; the rest stands.
- **Date**: 2026-11-15
- **Supersedes**: the parts of [0016](0016-the-browser-is-a-container-of-its-own.md) that gave the browser's web desktop an address of its own, a login of its own and a place behind the proxy's single sign-on (decisions 1 and 7 in part, the consequence "a sub-domain for the browser's page")
- **Spec**: D-25, F-CON-01, F-LGN; applies ADR 0004 (a dedicated account) and D-08

## Context

ADR 0016 made the browser a container of its own, that a person signs in to by hand and that Geminarr attaches to. It left the browser's web desktop as a page of its own: a sub-domain behind the single sign-on, and a login of the desktop on top. The first try of that design showed what the maintainer does not want:

- three identities in a row before Google's own (the proxy's single sign-on, the desktop's login, then the account), when the application is the one that should hold the right to reach the browser;
- a link that opened the browser on its default page (a search engine), not on the account's sign-in page;
- and, above all, a browser that is **reachable by something other than Geminarr**: a sub-domain and a login are an address and a door, and the maintainer wants none. The browser is a support that lets Geminarr reach the assistant without being taken for a bot; it serves that and nothing else.

## Decision

1. **The browser container is for Geminarr alone.** It publishes no port, has no Service, no ingress, no sub-domain, no sign-in of its own. It shares a network namespace with Geminarr (a pod; `network_mode: service:browser` in Compose); its debugging port (`9222`) and its web desktop (`3000`) listen there, **on the loopback**, and Geminarr reaches them as `127.0.0.1`. Both addresses must be loopback addresses (`GEMINARR__BROWSER__CDPURL`, `GEMINARR__BROWSER__DESKTOPURL`): the start is refused otherwise. **The image's desktop server listens on every interface by default** (measured), so an init script (`deploy/browser/loopback-only.sh`, mounted in `/custom-cont-init.d/`; a ConfigMap in the Kubernetes example) makes it listen on `127.0.0.1` only: without it, any other container of the network, or any pod of a cluster that does not enforce a network policy, could open a desktop that has no sign-in and is signed in to a Google account. Tested with the pinned image and, in the end-to-end suite, from another container of the network (8282 open as the control; 3000, 3001 and 9222 refused). **And Geminarr checks it itself, and fails closed**: it shares the namespace, so it reads every listening socket from `/proc/net/tcp` and `tcp6`; while anything but its own port listens off the loopback (a script that did not run, a new image that binds widely), `/browser/` answers 503, nothing is read through the browser, the heartbeat tells Mnemarr (an error, so that the operator hears of it where they look), the health says `browser-exposed`, and the runbook has the row. What cannot be checked (the listing cannot be read) is not taken for isolated.
2. **Geminarr relays the web desktop under `/browser/`**, behind its own sign-in (the operator's session, the fallback policy that closes every endpoint). A person is signed in to Geminarr once; the page *Connection* links to `/browser/`, in a tab of its own; the only credentials typed are the account's own, on the provider's own page. HTTP and WebSocket both pass. Geminarr neither types nor clicks in the desktop: it relays what the person does.
3. **What the relay forwards, and what it keeps apart.** **Only what the desktop's client uses, by a list** (`BrowserRelay.IsRelayed`): its page, its scripts, styles, fonts and pictures under `assets/`, a few files at the root (`selkies-core.js`, `manifest.json`, its icons), its WebSocket (`/browser/api/websockets`) and its status (`/browser/api/status`), in their plain form, by a read; no write over HTTP, and nothing else that the image's web server offers or may offer (its file listing, its other services, a development route: a `404`), because the pages of the desktop are served on Geminarr's own origin and a file that the browser saved must not be one of them. A list, and not a refusal of the known bad routes, so that a later version of the image that adds a route does not relay it silently: the desktop then shows a blank page, which is visible. The list is the contract with the image that was tested (its digest is in the examples). The request that goes on carries neither Geminarr's session cookie nor credentials; what comes back sets no cookie on Geminarr's origin; a WebSocket handshake that a page of another origin sends is refused (on top of `SameSite=Strict`): the origin is the public address (`GEMINARR__PUBLICURL`, so a proxy on a port of its own that sends no port in `Host` works), or the host and the port of the request, or, when a proxy sends a `Host` of its own, one of the allowed names on a default port; another port of the same host is another origin, and `*` in the allowed names proves nothing; **a WebSocket ends with the session that opened it** (it is checked every few seconds: signing out, or the end of the session's lifetime, closes the desktop); a service worker header of the desktop is dropped; **every request goes to the desktop with one origin, `https://geminarr.invalid`** (a reserved name that no web page can have), which the desktop is told to allow and nothing else (`SELKIES_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` of its image for the WebSocket, and rules in its nginx, added by the init script, for every request: the `Host` must be a loopback name (against DNS rebinding) and the origin Geminarr's, tested on the pinned image, any other gets a 403 whatever the method): a page that the browser itself loads shares its namespace and can reach the desktop on the loopback, and would otherwise get its screen and keyboard without a sign-in; a request without a session is sent to the sign-in page, and reaches the desktop for nobody. The pages of the desktop have their own content security policy (it draws on a canvas, decodes video, starts workers from blobs and talks over a WebSocket to this origin), given to that path only; they are never framed. Audio is not available: its worklet starts from a `data:` script, which the policy does not allow.
4. **The browser opens on the assistant** (`CHROME_CLI` of the image: the assistant's address), which sends a person who is not signed in to the provider's sign-in page: "Sign in" lands there, with nothing in between.
5. **The desktop needs a secure context** (it uses WebCodecs): HTTPS, or `localhost`. The reverse proxy in front of Geminarr terminates TLS as for any page; a plain-HTTP address on a network does not show the desktop.
6. **Kubernetes**: the only port a network policy lets in is Geminarr's (`8282`); `3000` and `9222` are no container port of the pod and no policy opens them, so another pod of the cluster cannot reach a desktop that has no sign-in.
7. **A new dependency**: `Yarp.ReverseProxy` (Microsoft's reverse proxy library), used for the relay only. A hand-written WebSocket relay would be less code than its tests are long; the library is the one the platform's own teams use for this. Its approval is the maintainer's (rule 12).

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the sub-domain and drop the desktop's login** (the single sign-on alone): rejected by the maintainer: an address and a door for something that is not meant to be reached by anything but Geminarr, and two identities instead of one.
- **A hand-written relay**: no new dependency, but a WebSocket relay that handles backpressure, close frames, upgrades and timeouts is the kind of code that is wrong in a corner; the library already has the tests of those corners.
- **Frame the desktop in Geminarr's page**: rejected: the desktop is another program's page with its own scripts; a frame would need the policy of Geminarr's whole interface to be loosened, and the relay's looser policy is better kept to one path.
- **Hide the browser behind a one-time link that Geminarr issues**: more machinery than the sign-in Geminarr already has, for the same effect.

## Consequences

- The deployment gets simpler: one Service, one address, one login to rotate, no sub-domain, no ingress rule and no secret for the browser. The examples of `deploy/`, `docs/network.md`, `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`, `docs/configuration.md` and the runbook say so in the same pull request.
- **The desktop's code runs on Geminarr's origin.** The relay serves another program's scripts (the image's web client) on the same origin as Geminarr's API, with the session cookie that the browser sends to that origin. A flaw in that client (a supply-chain compromise of the image, which is pinned by digest) could therefore act as the operator on Geminarr's API, including re-pointing the Mnemarr it sends to. The alternative, another origin for the relay, needs a second address to publish (cookies ignore ports; a sub-domain is another door), which the maintainer does not want; the policy of the path, the pin by digest and the dedicated account (ADR 0004) bound it. Said here so that it is a decision and not a surprise.
- **The security of the desktop is Geminarr's sign-in.** Whoever is signed in to Geminarr can use the browser, and with it the account: the operator's password is the key to the session. The relay is therefore the surface the review reads hardest (origin check, headers, no cookie in either direction, no way around the sign-in), and it is covered by integration tests (against a fake desktop and a real server for the WebSocket) and by the end-to-end journey (against a real Chromium container).
- **The egress of the pod** is unchanged by this decision and stays open for review (the price of the shared namespace, ADR 0016): the browser needs the provider's origins, and the pod's policy cannot tell the two containers apart. A proxy that lets through the provider's names only, between the browser and the network, would close it; it is a step of its own.
- The desktop is served under `/browser/` and the relay forwards the path unchanged, so the image must serve itself under exactly that path (`SUBFOLDER` of the linuxserver image, set in every example). A different image (Q-18) needs the same: a blank page and a `502` are what a mismatch looks like.
- A sandbox over plain HTTP on a network shows everything but the desktop; to see it, use HTTPS or `localhost`.
- Open: Q-17 (does the provider accept a program's read on a browser that a person signed in to?), Q-18 (which image, pinned by which digest).
