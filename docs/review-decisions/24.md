# Review decisions: pull request #24

> The browser is a container of its own, and Geminarr attaches to it (vertical 3, ADR 0016). Round 1 read head `b265b3d`; the answers below are on the head that follows it.

## R-24-1 | architecture | major | fixed

- **Finding**: `not-connected` cannot last once a browser is configured: a browser nobody signed in to reports signed-out, and the state turns `needs-login` after the passes.
- **Where**: `ConnectionTracker`, `WebReader`, SPEC §7.
- **Reason**: the behaviour is the intended one (a configured browser without a session *is* "sign in"); what was wrong was the wording. SPEC, the runbook and the reason texts now say when each of `not-connected` (no browser configured, or just disconnected) and `needs-login` (a browser, nobody signed in) is shown.

## R-24-2 | architecture | major | declined

- **Finding**: the pod-wide egress rule (any public address, port 443, minus the private ranges) also applies to Geminarr's own process, as a network policy cannot tell the two containers apart.
- **Where**: `deploy/kubernetes/geminarr.example.yaml`, `docs/network.md`, ADR 0016 ("price of the shared namespace").
- **Reason**: this is the price of the design the maintainer chose (a shared loopback instead of a debugging port on the network): the alternative is a debugging port reachable across the cluster, which is worse. The trade-off is written in ADR 0016 and `network.md`, and Geminarr's own process makes no call outside Mnemarr and the assistant (rule 7, enforced by code and tests, not by the policy). A name-based egress filter (a service mesh or an FQDN-aware policy) is the way to narrow it and is documented as such. It is a decision of the maintainer: accepted or refused.
- **Approved**: _awaiting the maintainer_

## R-24-3 | security | medium | declined

- **Finding**: same as R-24-2 (pod-wide egress covers Geminarr itself).
- **Where**: `deploy/kubernetes/geminarr.example.yaml`.
- **Reason**: see R-24-2; one decision, rated major by the architecture agent, so it is asked of the maintainer as such.

## R-24-4 | quality | major | fixed

- **Finding**: failures after the attachment (clearing cookies, reading cookie names) surface as `PlaywrightException`: a 500 on *Disconnect*, an unclassified failure in a pass.
- **Where**: `CdpBrowserHost`.
- **Reason**: every call on the attached browser now goes through `OnTheBrowserAsync`, which turns a dropped browser into a `ReaderException(browser-failed)` and logs only the kind of failure.

## R-24-5 | security | medium | fixed

- **Finding**: the browser container has no security context and no dropped capabilities.
- **Where**: `deploy/docker-compose.example.yml`, `deploy/kubernetes/geminarr.example.yaml`, e2e stack.
- **Reason**: it drops every capability but the six its init needs (`CHOWN`, `SETUID`, `SETGID`, `DAC_OVERRIDE`, `FOWNER`, `KILL`), takes no new privilege, and has memory, ephemeral-storage and shared-memory limits. Verified by starting the image with exactly these settings (Chromium runs, the debugging port answers) and by the end-to-end tests on the hardened container. Whether the image can run read-only or as non-root stays Q-18.
