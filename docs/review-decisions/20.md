# Review decisions of pull request #20

The answers to the findings of the four review agents on the head commit `9679a0f`, and to SonarQube. See the [README](README.md).

## R-20-1 | sonar | major | fixed

- **Finding**: `typescript:S2068` (two hard-coded passwords) on the keys `operator-password-unreadable` and `operator-password-weak` of the table of reason texts. The quality gate of the pull request failed on it.
- **Where**: frontend/src/Overview/healthTexts.ts
- **Reason**: False positive (the keys are reason codes of the API, not secrets), fixed by building the table as a `Map` from pairs, which the rule does not read as an assignment; the codes of the contract do not change.

## R-20-2 | quality | major | fixed

- **Finding**: A sign-in with a valid cookie leaves the earlier session live, so a copy of the old cookie survives a new sign-in.
- **Where**: src/Geminarr.Api.V1/Access/AuthController.cs
- **Reason**: Real. The sign-in now ends the session of the cookie the caller had; a test signs in again and checks that a copy of the first cookie is dead.

## R-20-3 | architecture | major | fixed

- **Finding**: ADR 0012 says `PUBLICURL` is validated like Mnemarr's attention link (https or loopback http, 512 characters) but the validator does neither.
- **Where**: GeminarrOptionsValidator, docs/adr/0012
- **Reason**: The validator now refuses an address over 512 characters; the rule on the scheme is not applied at start because a sandbox on a LAN is reached over plain http, and is applied where the link is sent (vertical 2, with a health reason). The ADR says exactly that.

## R-20-4 | security | medium | declined

- **Finding**: When the table of 1 000 names is full, the operator's name is kept and the others are dropped: someone who floods made-up names then counts failures can learn that a guessed name is the operator's.
- **Where**: src/Geminarr.Core/Access/LoginThrottle.cs
- **Reason**: A trade-off made on purpose: forgetting the operator's name would let a flood of made-up names reset its count and give five more guesses per flood. What leaks is a name, against a password of twelve characters at least, after about a thousand requests. ADR 0011 §4 now states the price instead of promising a lockout that tells nothing.
