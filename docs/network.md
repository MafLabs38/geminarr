# Network

Geminarr is **outbound only** towards the outside, and has **one inbound path**: its page, which must be behind a login.

## Inbound

One page, behind a login: Geminarr's own, and **a second origin for the browser's desktop** (a second port of Geminarr, opened by a ticket of the operator's session: ADR 0017, 0019). The browser container has no page, no port and no sign-in of its own.

| Port | Container | What | Who may reach it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8282/tcp | Geminarr | The page, the API and `/health/*` | The operator, **through the reverse proxy with TLS** (and its single sign-on if you have one), then Geminarr's own login. Probes (`/health/live`, `/health/ready`) may be reached by the platform. |
| 8283/tcp (`BROWSER__DESKTOPPORT`) | Geminarr | **The desktop's own origin**: the relay of the browser's web desktop (`/browser/`: its pages, its WebSocket and its status; no write, nothing else of the image's web server), and `/enter`, which takes a ticket. **Nothing else: no API, no interface.** Open only when a desktop is configured | The operator, **through the reverse proxy with TLS** like the page (a second host, or a second port of the same host; give its address as `GEMINARR__BROWSER__DESKTOPPUBLICURL` when the proxy gives it one). It answers 401 to anybody without the cookie that a ticket of the operator's session gives. **The desktop needs a secure context (HTTPS or `localhost`)**. |
| 3000/tcp (loopback) | Browser | The web desktop of the browser: the screen of an ordinary Chromium, where a person signs in to the **dedicated** Google account. No sign-in of its own | **Nobody but Geminarr**: it listens **on the loopback** of the network namespace that the two containers share (one pod; `network_mode: service:browser` in Compose), which an init script of the browser container makes true (`deploy/browser/loopback-only.sh`: the image listens on every interface otherwise) and which Geminarr checks itself (it reads the sockets of the namespace, and closes `/browser/` with the reason `browser-exposed` while anything but its own port listens off the loopback). It is published nowhere and in no Service, and no other container or pod can open it, whether or not a network policy is enforced. |
| 9222/tcp (loopback) | Browser | The debugging port of Chromium: **the key to the session** | **Nobody but Geminarr**, the same way. It is in no Service, no published port, no network policy. |

**The names the page may be reached by are a list** (`GEMINARR__ALLOWEDHOSTNAMES`, and the host of `GEMINARR__PUBLICURL`): a request for any other `Host` is refused with a 400, so that a web page that points its own name at this address (DNS rebinding) cannot read what the container says. The default is the loopback names; **add the name or the address you reach it by** (for example the address of the machine for a sandbox, or the host of the public address behind a proxy that keeps the `Host` header). The probes are exempt, because a platform calls them by the address of the pod.

Whoever is signed in to Geminarr can use the browser, and with it the account: **the operator's password is the key to the session**, and the desktop's origin is closed without a ticket of the operator's session (D-19, D-25, D-27): every call but the probes needs the operator's session, and the desktop's cookie is worth nothing on the page's origin. **The cookie of the page is sent by a browser to every port of the same host; the desktop's pages cannot read the answers of the API (another origin) and cannot write to it (the header of a write is never allowed to another origin).**

## Outbound (the list is the policy)

| From | Destination | Purpose | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| The browser container | The assistant's origin (`gemini.google.com`), the account provider's sign-in origin (`accounts.google.com`) and what Chromium itself reaches | A person signs in; a pass looks at the assistant's page; later the conversations are read | When a person uses it, and at every pass |
| Geminarr | The configured Mnemarr (HTTPS) | Send turns, positions, heartbeat; pairing and enrolment when the key is asked for | Every pass (a pairing: while it waits, a call every few seconds) |

Geminarr itself reaches nothing of the assistant: it asks the browser container, through its debugging address, to open the assistant's address in a page of its own (the only host it navigates to, `GEMINARR__BROWSER__ASSISTANTURL`; the one other page it opens, on the operator's click on Sign in, is the account provider's `GEMINARR__BROWSER__SIGNINURL`). What Chromium reaches is **the pod's network**, and a network policy applies to a pod, not to a container: so **the egress that the browser needs would also apply to Geminarr's own process** (the price of sharing a namespace so that the debugging port is never on a network, ADR 0016). That price is ended by **the egress guard** (ADR 0020): in the shared namespace, the process of Geminarr's user (uid 1654) may reach only Mnemarr's address and port, the loopback and the listed name servers, by rules that a one-shot container with `NET_ADMIN` sets before Geminarr starts (`deploy/egress/`; Geminarr has no capability and cannot change them). The browser's processes are not named and keep the pod's egress, and the network policy of the pod is where its list is held. **The guard does not cover what Geminarr could ask the browser to do over the debugging port**: whoever holds that port holds the browser's reach, so the pod's policy **by name** (Cilium `toFQDNs`, advised) is what bounds the browser, and Geminarr through it. Geminarr checks that the guard is there (a connection to an address that is on no list must be refused at once) and closes the desktop and the reading while it is not (`egress-unguarded`) (see the Kubernetes example: what a policy can say depends on the network plugin, and a policy knows addresses, not names). The exact list of hosts that the assistant's page needs is written down by spike S1 and **a new destination is a decision** (ADR 0008: the network policy is in the trust anchor; ADR 0016).

A proxy in front of Geminarr: it must pass WebSocket upgrades without buffering them (the relayed desktop uses one, under `/browser/api/websockets` of its own origin); it must publish the desktop's origin (port 8283, or a host of its own) as well as the page's; and it must not cache `/api`. Check that the `Upgrade` and `Connection` headers are sent once: the desktop answers 204 to a request whose `Upgrade` header is not exactly `websocket`, which a proxy that adds them twice produces (a reverse proxy with its own WebSocket template may do it). Set `GEMINARR__PUBLICURL` to the address that people use (with its port if it is not the default one), and `GEMINARR__BROWSER__DESKTOPPUBLICURL` to the desktop's (an origin, no path): the `Origin` of the desktop's WebSocket is judged against the second, and against the `Host` of the request, and a proxy that sends a `Host` of its own needs that name in `GEMINARR__ALLOWEDHOSTNAMES`.

## Firewalls and proxies at the other end

Geminarr reaches Mnemarr over HTTPS only (a loopback address or an explicit sandbox switch, `GEMINARR__MNEMARR__ALLOWINSECUREHTTP`, excepted); redirects are not followed, every call is bounded (30 seconds), and the key goes only to the address that was configured. Which of Mnemarr's paths must be open to it: `/api/v1/ingest/*` (conversations, positions, heartbeat, pairing, enrolment). Mnemarr's own page, where a person approves a pairing, is a link that Geminarr shows with its host visible: Geminarr never calls it.
